# Staff Training Automation
 
A web-based staff manual designed for a busy supermarket environment, where high staff turnover makes consistent training difficult.
 
## Background
 
The supermarket where I work is located near a university. Many employees are international students — some return to their home countries when their visas or studies come to an end, while others move on to full-time jobs after graduation.
 
This means new employees need to be recruited and trained regularly. But in a busy shop floor, it's difficult to have someone walk a new starter through each task step by step. Training is often passed on verbally, and the quality depends on who provides it. New staff end up asking the same questions repeatedly — or worse, not asking at all and making mistakes they were never taught to avoid.
 
## What I Built
 
A mobile-friendly web app with step-by-step photo guides for common till operations:
 
- **Payment** (Card / Cash)
- **Refund** (Card / Cash)
- **Closing** (Night)
Each guide uses real photos of the till screen with on-screen annotations, presented as swipeable slides. The idea is that a new starter can pull it up on their phone and follow along without needing someone standing next to them.
 
Hosted on Cloudflare Workers — no login, no install, just a link.
 
## What I Learned
 
In an operational environment, transferring knowledge to new staff is a real challenge — especially where turnover is high. Small organisations often can't afford to dedicate resources to onboarding. The available workforce is already limited, and pulling someone out to serve as a full-time training lead for employees who may leave within months is rarely practical.
 
Working in a supermarket that sits in exactly this context, I came to appreciate two things: how difficult knowledge transfer actually is, and how much depends on incentive design — creating conditions where staff are motivated to learn and adopt new information.
 
If the effort of learning something new isn't connected to a clear incentive, and if not knowing it doesn't lead to any immediate discomfort or risk, people won't seek it out. That gap compounds: when frontline staff don't absorb what the management team is trying to implement, initiatives lose momentum — not because of resistance, but because of indifference.
 
This project gave me a chance to observe and think about those dynamics firsthand.
